# IT Risk & Control Self-Assessment (RCSA)

## 1. Overview

Risk and Control Self-Assessment (RCSA) is a structured process used by organizations to identify risks within business processes, evaluate existing controls, assess control effectiveness, and determine whether additional risk treatment is required.

This section documents the practical application of RCSA principles to IT and technology-related processes.

> **Note:** The examples in this repository are fictional and intended for educational and professional portfolio purposes.

---

## 2. Purpose of the IT RCSA

The IT RCSA is intended to help identify and assess risks associated with technology processes and determine whether existing controls adequately manage those risks.

The RCSA process supports:

* Risk identification
* Control identification
* Control effectiveness assessment
* Inherent risk assessment
* Residual risk assessment
* Risk treatment
* Control improvement
* Risk monitoring

---

## 3. RCSA Methodology

The RCSA process follows:

**Process → Risk → Control → Assessment → Treatment → Monitoring**

### Step 1 — Identify the Process

Determine the IT process being assessed.

Examples:

* Access Management
* Change Management
* Vulnerability Management
* Incident Management
* Backup & Recovery
* Asset Management
* Employee Awareness

### Step 2 — Identify the Risk

Determine what could go wrong within the process.

### Step 3 — Identify Existing Controls

Document the controls that are intended to prevent, detect, or correct the identified risk.

### Step 4 — Assess Control Effectiveness

Evaluate whether the controls are appropriately designed and operating effectively.

### Step 5 — Assess Inherent Risk

Assess the risk before considering the effectiveness of existing controls.

### Step 6 — Assess Residual Risk

Determine the level of risk remaining after considering existing controls.

### Step 7 — Determine Treatment

Determine whether the risk should be:

* Mitigated
* Accepted
* Transferred
* Avoided

### Step 8 — Define Actions

Where additional treatment is required, document specific remediation actions, owners, and target dates.

## 6. RCSA Control Types

Controls can generally be categorized as:

### Preventive Controls

Designed to prevent a risk event from occurring.

Examples:

* Access restrictions
* MFA
* Segregation of duties
* Approval requirements

### Detective Controls

Designed to identify an event after it occurs.

Examples:

* Security monitoring
* Audit logs
* Access reviews
* Vulnerability scans

### Corrective Controls

Designed to correct or recover from an event.

Examples:

* Account remediation
* Incident response
* System restoration
* Patch deployment

---

## 7. RCSA Key Principle

An effective RCSA should not simply ask:

> "Do we have a control?"

It should ask:

> **"Does the control adequately address the risk, and is it operating effectively?"**

The objective is to understand the organization's actual risk exposure and identify areas requiring additional treatment.

## 9. Key Takeaway

RCSA connects risk management theory to actual business processes.

The goal is to understand:

> **What process are we assessing? → What can go wrong? → What controls exist? → Are the controls effective? → What is the remaining risk? → What action is required?**
