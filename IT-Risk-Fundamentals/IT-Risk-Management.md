# IT Risk Management

## 1. Overview

IT Risk Management is the process of identifying, assessing, treating, and monitoring risks arising from the use, operation, management, and dependence on information technology.

IT Risk Management helps organizations understand how technology-related events could affect business objectives and ensures that appropriate controls are implemented to manage those risks.

## 2. Why IT Risk Management Matters

Organizations increasingly depend on technology to support business operations. Weaknesses in technology processes, systems, infrastructure, or controls can therefore result in:

* Financial losses
* Operational disruptions
* Unauthorized access
* Data loss or exposure
* Regulatory and compliance issues
* Fraud
* Reputational damage

Effective IT Risk Management helps organizations identify these risks before they result in significant business impact.

## 3. IT Risk Management Lifecycle

A typical IT Risk Management lifecycle consists of:

**Identify → Assess → Treat → Monitor → Report**

### Identify

Identify technology-related risks arising from systems, processes, people, infrastructure, third parties, or changes within the organization.

### Assess

Evaluate the likelihood and potential impact of each identified risk.

Risk assessment may consider:

* Likelihood
* Impact
* Existing controls
* Inherent risk
* Residual risk

### Treat

Determine how the organization will respond to the risk.

Common treatment options include:

* Mitigate
* Accept
* Transfer
* Avoid

### Monitor

Continuously monitor identified risks, controls, remediation actions, and changes in the risk environment.

### Report

Communicate significant risks, control weaknesses, remediation progress, and emerging risks to relevant stakeholders.

## 4. Common IT Risk Categories

Examples of IT Risk categories include:

| Risk Category            | Example                                        |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Access Management        | Excessive user privileges                      |
| Vulnerability Management | Unpatched vulnerabilities                      |
| Change Management        | Unauthorized system changes                    |
| Incident Management      | Delayed response to security incidents         |
| Asset Management         | Incomplete IT asset inventory                  |
| Backup & Recovery        | Failure to recover critical systems            |
| Third-Party Risk         | Security weaknesses at service providers       |
| Data Protection          | Unauthorized exposure of sensitive information |
| Infrastructure Risk      | Failure of critical technology infrastructure  |
| Business Continuity      | Inability to maintain critical IT services     |

## 5. IT Risk vs Cybersecurity Risk

IT Risk is broader than cybersecurity risk.

**IT Risk** covers risks arising from technology, systems, processes, infrastructure, people, and technology-related dependencies.

**Cybersecurity Risk** focuses more specifically on threats to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information and technology environments.

Cybersecurity risk is therefore an important component of the broader IT Risk landscape.

## 6. Practical IT Risk Example

### Scenario

A system administrator uses a generic administrative account to make changes to a production system.

### Risk

The use of a shared generic account may prevent the organization from reliably identifying the individual who performed a system change.

### Potential Impact

* Reduced accountability
* Inability to establish individual responsibility
* Unauthorized or inappropriate system changes
* Audit findings
* Increased operational and security risk

### Recommended Controls

* Use uniquely assigned named user accounts.
* Enforce appropriate privileged access management.
* Implement dual custody where appropriate.
* Enable detailed activity logging.
* Review privileged activities periodically.

### Risk Treatment

**Mitigate**

The organization should strengthen privileged access controls and eliminate unnecessary use of generic administrative accounts.

## 7. Key Takeaway

IT Risk Management is not simply about identifying problems. It is a continuous process of understanding technology-related risks, evaluating their potential effect on business objectives, implementing appropriate controls, and monitoring whether those controls remain effective.
